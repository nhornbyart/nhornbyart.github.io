Dreams & Dreaming

Basic Dream Facts

Content Constraints

  • Only dream about what we know
  • Based on what we've seen/experienced before
  • Can't imagine entirely novel colors or sensations
  • Dreams remix existing memories and experiences

Why We Forget Dreams

  • Brain chemistry: Low norepinephrine during REM (memory hormone)
  • Rapid fading: Dreams fade quickly upon waking
  • No effort to encode: Dreams aren't consciously reinforced
  • Abrupt transitions: Waking jars us from narrative

Lucid Dreaming

Definition: Dreaming while knowing/being aware that you are dreaming

How It Works

  • Bring conscious awareness into the dream
  • "Wake up" within the dream without waking up physically
  • Retain some executive control

Developing Lucid Dreaming

Technique Description
Reality checks Throughout day, ask "Am I dreaming?"
Dream journals Record dreams upon waking to notice patterns
MILD technique Go to sleep with intention: "I will know I'm dreaming"
Wake-back-to-bed Wake after 5-6 hours, stay awake 20 min, return to sleep
Recognize signs Impossible physics, shifting environments, odd details

Benefits of Lucid Dreaming

Interesting Side Effect

  • Beginning to notice dream details → higher level of awareness in waking state as well
  • Enhanced metacognition (thinking about thinking)
  • Better recognition of patterns and inconsistencies

Daydreaming

Study: 45 undergraduates tested for creativity

Experimental Setup

Remarkable Findings

  • Boring task group: 41% more creative uses
  • Why? Allowed mind to "incubate" the problem
  • Moderate cognitive load (not engaging, but not idle) optimal for creativity

Purpose of Daydreaming

Function Description
Problem-solving Subconscious works on problems
Incubation Ideas develop outside conscious attention
Creativity Novel connections made
Planning Mental simulation of future scenarios
Emotion regulation Process emotions, fantasize about scenarios

The Goldilocks Zone

  • Too much focus: Stuck in conscious constraints
  • Too little focus: Random thought, no progress
  • Just right: Daydreaming allows productive incubation
