Humanistic Personality Theory

Humanistic Personality Theory

Core Principle: People naturally strive for self-actualization (achieving their unique potential)

Self-Actualization

Definition: Fulfilling one's unique potential; becoming the best version of oneself

Key Humanistic Theorists

Abraham Maslow

Hierarchy of Needs

Personality Implications:

  • People with unmet lower needs → anxious, focused on survival
  • People with met basic needs → can pursue growth
  • Self-actualization is highest human motivation

Carl Rogers

Focus: "The Self" and client-centered therapy

The Self (Rogers' Concept)

Definition: Person's perception of their own nature, abilities, and worth

Components

Positive Regard

Definition: Evaluation of yourself based on how others respond to you

Conditions of Worth

Definition: Specific conditions you must meet to feel valued by important people

Unconditional Positive Regard

Definition: Perception that significant others value you completely, without condition

Impact on Personality

  • With unconditional regard: Authentic, confident, actualized
  • With conditional regard: Self-conscious, anxious, inauthentic
  • Parenting role: Provide foundation for healthy personality

Self-Actualized Personality

Traits of Self-Actualized People

Characteristics

  • Realistic: See self and world accurately (not distorted)
  • Accepting: Acknowledge limitations and imperfections
  • Natural: Don't pretend; be authentic
  • Creative: Solve problems in novel ways
  • Independent: Don't need constant approval
  • Purposeful: Work toward meaningful goals

Humanistic Therapy

Core Principles

Therapist Role

  • Empathetic listener
  • Non-judgmental
  • Provide unconditional positive regard
  • Reflect back what they hear
  • Help client find own answers

Strengths & Limitations

Strengths

  • ✓ Emphasizes potential for growth
  • ✓ Explains motivation for achievement
  • ✓ Supportive approach proves effective
  • ✓ Respects individual differences

Limitations

  • ✗ Vague concepts (hard to define, measure)
  • ✗ Overly optimistic about human nature
  • ✗ Limited empirical research
  • ✗ Doesn't account for mental illness biology
  • ✗ "Self-actualization" looks different for everyone
  • ✗ Assumes everyone can overcome obstacles with support
