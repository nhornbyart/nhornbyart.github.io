Motor Development

Definition: Maturation — internally programmed growth of a child; plays a key role in progress of motor skills

What is Maturation?

Sequence of Motor Development

Gross Motor Milestones

Key Principle: Cephalocaudal (head to tail) development

Fine Motor Milestones

Key Principle: Proximodistal (near to far) development

Role of Maturation vs. Experience

Maturation's Role

  • Determines readiness: Baby must be neurologically ready
  • Sets sequence: Order is largely fixed
  • Provides capability: Brain and muscles must be capable

Experience's Role

  • Accelerates slightly: Practice helps, but only after ready
  • Cannot reverse order: Can't crawl before trunk control ready
  • Refines skills: Once capable, practice improves ability

The Critical Window

  • Too early: Child not ready, cannot do skill
  • Right time: Skill emerges naturally with practice
  • After maturation: Still develops, but more effortful

Factors Supporting Motor Development

Environmental Factors

  • Space to move: Room to explore
  • Safe environment: Freedom without danger
  • Opportunities: Varied surfaces, objects to manipulate
  • Encouragement: Adult support and attention

Physical Factors

  • Nutrition: Adequate protein and calories
  • Sleep: Needed for neurological development
  • Exercise: Movement strengthens muscles, builds connections

Parental Involvement

  • Tummy time: Strengthens neck and shoulder muscles
  • Play: Encourages practice of emerging skills
  • Safety: Protection from obstacles while exploring
  • Responsiveness: Motivation to move and explore

Individual Differences

Normal Variation

  • Some babies walk at 10 months, others at 15 months
  • All within normal range
  • Usually not predictive of later abilities
  • Genetics influence development rate

Early Signs of Concern

  • Not rolling by 6 months
  • Not sitting by 9 months
  • Not walking by 18 months
  • Significant asymmetry (one side lagging)

Why Early Identification Matters

  • Early intervention can help
  • Brain plasticity highest in infancy
  • Small delays can become larger gaps without support
