Frontal Lobe

Frontal Lobe

Location: Front of brain (largest lobe, about 1/3 of cerebral cortex)

Motor Cortex

Location: Precentral gyrus (strip running vertically near front)

Function: Initiates and controls voluntary movements

Motor Homunculus (Motor Map)

Broca's Area (Language Production)

Location: Left frontal lobe (inferior frontal gyrus)

Function: Speech production and language expression

Example of Broca's Aphasia

Normal speech: "I went to the store and bought milk"
Broca's: "I... store... buy... milk"
(Function words like "to", "and", "the" are missing)

Personality & Behavior

Personality Networks

  • Sense of self
  • Social behavior
  • Emotional regulation
  • Motivation
  • Risk assessment

Case Study: Phineas Gage

1848 Railroad Accident:

  • Metal tamping iron pierced through left frontal lobe
  • Survived with minimal immediate neurological damage
  • Dramatic personality changes:
    • Responsible → irresponsible
    • Polite → crude/profane
    • Good-natured → irritable
    • Cautious → reckless

Significance: First clear evidence that damage to specific brain region causes specific behavioral changes

Working Memory

  • Capacity: Hold 4-7 items temporarily
  • Duration: 15-30 seconds without rehearsal
  • Function: Essential for reasoning, problem-solving, reading comprehension
  • Damage effect: Difficulty with complex tasks

Damage Effects

Damage Location Effects
Motor cortex Paralysis (contralateral)
Broca's area Speech difficulty, understanding preserved
Prefrontal cortex Personality/behavior changes, poor judgment
Executive regions Planning, attention, impulse control problems

Neuroplasticity & Recovery

  • Frontal lobe has good recovery potential
  • Other regions may compensate for motor loss
  • Language recovery possible (especially in young people)
  • Rehabilitation can rebuild connections
