Occipital Lobe

Occipital Lobe

Location: Very back of brain (posterior/occipital pole)

Primary Visual Cortex (V1)

Location: First visual processing area (back of occipital lobe)

Function: Analyzes basic visual features

Visual Processing Hierarchy

What Gets Processed

Feature Processing
Color Wavelength analysis by color-sensitive neurons
Form Edge detection, shape recognition
Motion Direction and speed of movement
Brightness Luminance contrast
Depth Binocular disparity (both eyes' input)
Location Retinotopic mapping (where in visual field)

Retinotopic Organization

Principle: Visual field maps directly onto visual cortex

Result: Cortex contains distorted "map" of visual world

Visual Pathways to Higher Processing

"WHAT" Pathway

  • Route: Occipital → Temporal lobe
  • Function: Object identification
  • Question: "What am I looking at?"
  • Example: Recognizing a face as your friend

"WHERE" Pathway

  • Route: Occipital → Parietal lobe
  • Function: Spatial location and movement
  • Question: "Where is it / where is it going?"
  • Example: Tracking a moving car

Damage Effects

Damage to V1

  • Scotoma: Blind spot in visual field (often triangular)
  • Loss is retinotopic: Corresponds to location of damage
  • Rest of vision: Usually preserved

Visual Agnosia (Occipital Connection Issues)

  • Can see all basic features
  • Cannot recognize what they're looking at
  • "Visual object agnosia" = see but don't recognize
  • Damage not to V1 but to connections to temporal lobe

Cortical Blindness

  • Massive bilateral occipital damage
  • Can lead to complete blindness
  • Some patients have "blindsight" (detect movement without awareness)

Color Processing

Color-Selective Areas

  • Specific regions respond to specific colors
  • V4 area specialized for color
  • Color perception involves occipital + temporal processing

Color Blindness

  • Usually retinal (cone cells lack pigment)
  • Rarely due to cortical damage alone

Motion Processing

Motion Areas

  • Middle temporal area (MT/V5) processes motion
  • Detects direction and speed
  • Essential for tracking moving objects

Damage Effects

  • Akinetopsia: Motion blindness
  • Can't see movement (see "snapshots")
  • Extremely rare with cortical damage
  • Dangerous (can't judge approaching cars)

Clinical Significance

Migraine with Aura

  • Occipital lobe hyperactivity
  • Visual disturbances (zigzag lines, blind spots)
  • Followed by headache

Occipital Seizures

  • Visual hallucinations
  • Flashing lights, geometric patterns
  • Often start in back of visual field
