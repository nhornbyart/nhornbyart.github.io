Sensory Vulnerability

Key Point: Newborns have innate sensory filtering mechanisms to prevent overwhelm

Basic Reflexes

Survival Reflexes

Rooting Reflex

  • Trigger: Light touch on cheek
  • Response: Head turns toward touch, mouth opens
  • Purpose: Helps baby find breast/bottle
  • Duration: Present at birth, fades around 3-4 months

Grasping Reflex

  • Trigger: Something placed in palm
  • Response: Baby grips tightly
  • Strength: Surprisingly strong for newborn
  • Duration: Present at birth, fades around 5-6 months

Sucking Reflex

  • Trigger: Anything touches roof of mouth
  • Response: Automatic sucking motion
  • Purpose: Feeding
  • Duration: Present at birth, becomes voluntary around 2-3 months

Moro Reflex (Startle)

  • Trigger: Sudden movement or loud noise
  • Response: Arms fling out, then curl back in
  • Purpose: Possible evolutionary protection
  • Duration: Present at birth, fades by 5-6 months

Sensory Abilities

Vision

  • At birth: Sees light and dark, blurry
  • Color: Can distinguish some colors
  • Focus: Best at 8-12 inches
  • Preference: Faces and high contrast

Hearing

  • In womb: Can hear sounds from 6-7 months gestation
  • At birth: Responds to loud sounds
  • Preference: Higher pitched sounds (voices)
  • Startle: Sudden loud noises trigger Moro reflex

Taste & Smell

  • Taste: Prefer sweet (breast milk is sweet)
  • Smell: Recognize mother's scent
  • Response: Turn toward familiar smells

Touch

  • Sensitivity: More sensitive than adults to pain
  • Temperature: Can sense temperature changes
  • Texture: Respond to soft vs. rough surfaces

Attachment & Bonding

Critical for Survival

  • Need caregiver contact and responsiveness
  • Physical touch essential for development
  • Emotional connection supports growth

Comfort Behaviors

  • Hold close to provide security
  • Responsive caregiving reduces stress
  • Physical contact regulates temperature and heart rate
