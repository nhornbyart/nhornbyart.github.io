Trait Theory

Trait Theory

Core Principle: Personality is best understood as a set of measurable traits; everyone possesses all traits to varying degrees

Fundamental Assumptions

Example

Everyone has:

  • Some level of extraversion (more or less outgoing)
  • Some level of conscientiousness (more or less organized)
  • Some level of agreeableness (more or less sympathetic)

Your unique combination creates your personality

Gordon Allport (First Trait Theorist)

Trait Categories

Allport identified over 4,000 trait-related words in English!

Cardinal Trait

  • Definition: Trait so strong, person is identified with it
  • Rarity: Very rare
  • Example: "Machiavellian" (though not from Machiavelli himself)
  • Effect: Dominates personality entirely

Central Trait

  • Definition: Traits that best describe a person
  • Number: Usually 5-10 main traits
  • Example: Honest, responsible, cheerful
  • Effect: Core to personality

Secondary Trait

  • Definition: Traits less consistent; show situationally
  • Context-dependent: Only appear in specific situations
  • Example: Shy at parties but talkative with close friends
  • Effect: Less central to personality

How Traits Are Measured

Rating Scales

"Rate yourself 1-7 on each trait"

Trait Scale
Extraversion 1 (Very Introverted) ← → 7 (Very Extraverted)
Conscientiousness 1 (Very Disorganized) ← → 7 (Very Organized)
Agreeableness 1 (Difficult) ← → 7 (Very Sympathetic)

Self-Report Questionnaires

  • Person rates themselves
  • Scored objectively
  • Produces numerical scores
  • Reliable and consistent

Observer Ratings

  • Others rate the person
  • Can compare to self-report
  • Reveals blind spots
  • More objective perspective

Advantages of Trait Theory

Strength Over Earlier Approaches

Strengths

  • ✓ Objective measurement
  • ✓ Scientific approach
  • ✓ Predictive validity
  • ✓ Cross-cultural research
  • ✓ Applied uses (job selection, compatibility)

Limitations

Criticisms of Trait Theory

  • ✗ Oversimplifies personality
  • ✗ Ignores situational factors
  • ✗ Doesn't explain why traits exist
  • ✗ Assumes stability (people change)
  • ✗ Self-report bias (people not always accurate)
  • ✗ Doesn't address motivation, values
  • ✗ Cultural differences in trait expression

The Lexical Hypothesis

Idea: Most important traits are encoded in language

Process:

  1. Find all personality-describing words in dictionary
  2. Reduce to single traits (synonyms combined)
  3. Ask people to rate themselves
  4. Use statistics to find patterns
  5. Result: Core personality dimensions

Finding: Different researchers found different numbers of core traits

  • Two factors: Introversion-Extraversion
  • Five factors: "Big Five" (most popular)
  • Sixteen factors: Myers-Briggs
  • Others: 3, 6, 7, 10 factors

Trait Theory Applications

Career Counseling

  • Match personality traits to job requirements
  • "This job needs high conscientiousness"

Compatibility Assessment

  • Compare couple's traits
  • Identify potential conflicts

Clinical Psychology

  • Trait extremes suggest personality disorders
  • High neuroticism → anxiety, depression
  • Low agreeableness → antagonistic behavior

Organizational Psychology

  • Team composition
  • Leadership potential
  • Employee selection
