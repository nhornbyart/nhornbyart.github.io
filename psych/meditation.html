Meditation

Meditation

Definition: Focusing of attention to clear one's mind and produce relaxation

Transcendental Meditation

Approach: Mental repetition of mantra

Practice

  • Sit with eyes closed
  • Mentally repeat a mantra (e.g., "Om")
  • Continue for 15-20 minutes
  • Practice 2× per day

How It Works

  1. Mantra absorbs attention
  2. Reduces random thought patterns
  3. Induces deep relaxation response
  4. Regular practice builds neurological changes

Benefits

  • Deep relaxation
  • Reduced anxiety
  • Improved focus
  • Sense of inner peace

Mindfulness Meditation

Approach: Focus on present moment awareness

Practice

  • Observe body sensations (tension, comfort)
  • Focus on breath (natural rhythm)
  • Notice thoughts without judgment
  • Return attention when mind wanders

Key Principle

  • Non-judgmental observation
  • Accept what arises without trying to change it
  • Develop awareness of mental patterns

Benefits

  • Enhanced present-moment awareness
  • Reduced rumination
  • Better emotional regulation
  • Decreased anxiety

Breath Meditation

Approach: Concentrate on breathing process

Practice

  • Focus exclusively on breath
  • Notice inhale and exhale
  • Count breaths or use simple pattern (4-7-8)
  • Use breath as anchor for attention

Benefits

  • Immediate calming effect
  • Portable (can do anywhere)
  • Provides focus point
  • Develops concentration
  • Activates parasympathetic nervous system

Comparison

Physiological Changes During Meditation

  • Brain waves: Shift toward alpha/theta rhythms
  • Heart rate: Decreases
  • Blood pressure: Lowers
  • Cortisol: Reduced (stress hormone)
  • Parasympathetic activation: Increased
  • Brain plasticity: Long-term meditators show structural changes
