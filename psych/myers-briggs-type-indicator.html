Myers-Briggs Type Indicator

Myers-Briggs Type Indicator (MBTI)

Based on Carl Jung's personality types; popular but scientifically controversial

Historical Basis

The Four Dimensions

Dimension 1: ATTITUDE

How you interact with the world

Dimension 2: PERCEPTION

How you gather information

Dimension 3: JUDGMENT

How you make decisions

Dimension 4: STRUCTURE

How you organize life

16 Personality Types

Combination of 4 dimensions = 16 types

E/I × S/N × T/F × J/P = 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 = 16 combinations

MBTI Strengths

Popular Applications

  • ✓ Widely used in career counseling
  • ✓ Fun, engaging, self-insight
  • ✓ Provides framework for understanding differences
  • ✓ Good conversation starter
  • ✓ Many online resources

Why People Like It

  • Easy to remember 4-letter type
  • Validates personal differences
  • Suggests career paths
  • Explains relationship dynamics
  • Non-judgmental framework

MBTI Criticisms

Scientific Issues

Major Limitations

  • ✗ Low test-retest reliability: Different results on repeat
  • ✗ Limited predictive validity: Doesn't predict outcomes well
  • ✗ Dichotomous thinking: Forces choices (I/E) that aren't binary
  • ✗ Insufficient research: Far fewer studies than Big Five
  • ✗ Overstated simplicity: People claim certain behaviors impossible for their type
  • ✗ Misuse as excuse: "I'm an INTJ so I'm blunt" (deterministic)

MBTI vs. Big Five

Comparison

Factor MBTI Big Five

MBTI as Tool vs. Science

As a Self-Reflection Tool

  • ✓ Good for thinking about preferences
  • ✓ Useful in career exploration
  • ✓ Conversation about personality
  • ✓ Personal insight

As Scientific Assessment

  • ✗ Not rigorously validated
  • ✗ Unreliable over time
  • ✗ Can't predict behavior well
  • ✗ Oversimplifies personality

Current Status

Controversy

  • Widely popular in business/counseling
  • Criticized by personality psychologists
  • Not supported as diagnostic tool
  • Used despite scientific limitations

Legitimate Uses

  • Career exploration starting point
  • Team awareness building
  • General self-reflection
  • Ice breaker activities

Not Recommended For

  • Clinical diagnosis
  • Personnel selection (legally risky)
  • Serious psychological assessment
  • Major life decisions

Key Takeaway

MBTI is entertaining and can prompt self-reflection, but it's not scientifically rigorous. For serious personality assessment, the Big Five is more reliable and researched.

Think of MBTI as a fun personality framework that captures something real about how people differ, but not as psychological truth the way Big Five is.
