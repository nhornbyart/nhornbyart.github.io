Sensory Adaptation & Signal Detection

Sensory Adaptation

Definition: Getting used to a constant level of stimulation and only responding to changes away from that baseline

Purpose of Sensory Adaptation

Purpose Example
Survival Focus on threats, ignore constant background
Prevent Overstimulation Brain doesn't get overwhelmed by unchanging input
Conserve Energy Allocate attention to novel/important stimuli

Examples by Sense

Signal-Detection Theory

Definition: Our ability to detect stimuli depends on motivation, skill, and competing stimuli — not a fixed threshold

Key Argument

  • Challenges: The traditional idea of absolute thresholds
  • Proposes: Detection is influenced by psychological factors, not just sensory ability

Factors Influencing Detection

Factor Effect
High Motivation Increases hits (and false alarms)
Expertise/Skill Better discrimination between signal and noise
Competing Stimuli More noise = harder to detect signal
Expectation Primed senses detect more easily
Cost of Miss vs. False Alarm Adjust sensitivity based on consequences

Real-World Application

Airport Security Screening:

  • High motivation to catch threats
  • Competing stimuli (thousands of bags)
  • Cost of miss >> cost of false alarm
  • → Results in lower threshold (more false alarms)

Pre-Attentive vs. Attentive Processing

Pre-Attentive (Automatic)

  • Happen unconsciously
  • Very fast
  • Don't require effort
  • Process multiple stimuli in parallel
  • Example: Noticing your name in a crowded room

Attentive (Careful)

  • Require conscious attention
  • Slower
  • Require mental effort
  • Process one thing at a time (mostly)
  • Example: Reading a difficult textbook passage
