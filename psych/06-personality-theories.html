06 Personality Theories

Personality Theories

Assessment Methods

Projective Tests

Tests where individuals interpret ambiguous stimuli, revealing their personality

Rorschach Inkblots

  • Interpretation of inkblot images
  • Reveals unconscious processes and personality patterns

TAT (Thematic Aperception Test)

  • Individuals create stories about ambiguous images
  • Reveals themes, concerns, and motivations

Psychoanalytic Theory

Carl Jung

Personal vs. Collective Unconscious

  • Personal Unconscious: Individual repressed thoughts and experiences
  • Collective Unconscious: Inherited instincts, urges, and memories of all humans

Archetypes

  • Definition: Inherited ideas based on ancestors' experiences
  • Function: Shape one's perception of the world
  • Examples: The Hero, The Shadow, The Wise Old Man

Albert Bandura

Bobo Doll Experiment

  • Looked for personality of an aggressive person
  • Demonstrated that aggression can be learned through observation and modeling

Cognitive Theory

Core Principle

Ideas of oneself, of others, and of the world shape personalities

Schemas

  • Definition: Mental representation of people, events, and concepts
  • Function: Determine how we perceive things and respond to them

Humanistic Theory

Core Concept

Strive for self-actualization (one's unique potential)

Key Theorists

Abraham Maslow

  • Developed concept of self-actualization
  • Believed in human potential and growth

Carl Rogers

  • Focused on "The Self"
  • Emphasized unconditional positive regard

Self-Actualized Traits

  • Realistically oriented
  • Accept the world as it is
  • Spontaneous
  • Creative
  • Resist conformity
  • Independent

Carl Rogers's "The Self"

Positive Regard

  • Definition: View of yourself based on positive interactions
  • Conditions of worth: Conditions one must meet to feel positivity about oneself
  • Unconditional positive regard: Perception that significant others value you in your entirety

Trait Theory

Core Principles

  1. Everyone possesses all traits
  2. Traits can be quantified
  3. The amount of each trait determines your personality

Gordon Allport (First Trait Theorist)

Trait Categories

Trait Type Definition Frequency
Cardinal Trait So strong, person is identified with it Rare
Central Trait Best describes a person Common
Secondary Trait Less consistent preferences Situational

The Big Five Personality Traits

Most well-known trait theory — central for all people

OCEAN Model

Trait Spectrum
Openness Open-mindedness ←→ Close-mindedness
Conscientiousness Responsible ←→ Careless
Extroversion Outgoing ←→ Reserved
Agreeableness Sympathetic ←→ Cruel
Neuroticism (Emotional Stability) Moody ←→ Calm/Relaxed

Myers-Briggs Personality Test (MBTI)

Basis

Based on Carl Jung's writings

Four Dimensions

Jung presented 4 ways in which people experience the world:

Dimension Poles
Attitude Extroversion ←→ Introversion
Perception Sensing ←→ Intuition
Judgment Thinking ←→ Feeling
Structure Judging ←→ Perception

Results

  • 16 types total (2^4 combinations)
  • Preferences: Not better or worse; just different styles

Common Types

Examples: INTJ, ENFP, ISTJ, ENFJ, etc.
