Cognitive Personality Theory

Cognitive Personality Theory

Core Principle: Ideas of oneself, others, and the world shape personalities

How Cognition Shapes Personality

Schemas

Definition: Mental representation of people, events, and concepts

What Are Schemas?

  • Mental templates: Internal organization of information
  • Patterns: Recognizable sequences or categories
  • Organization: How we structure knowledge
  • Predictive: Help us anticipate what comes next

Types of Schemas

Type Example
Person schema Mental image of "teacher" or "boss"
Event schema Knowledge of how dinner party unfolds
Concept schema Understanding of "honesty" or "friendship"
Self-schema Beliefs about yourself

How Schemas Influence Personality

Schema Activation

Example: "Critical Person" Schema

Person has schema: "People are judgmental"

Situation Perception Interpretation Response
Colleague quiet in meeting Notices them looking "They think I'm stupid" Becomes defensive
Boss asks question Focuses on tone "They're criticizing me" Gets anxious
Friend laughs Thinks it's at them "They find me funny/weird" Withdraws

Reality vs. Schema

  • Same situation, different schemas = different personalities
  • Schemas can be accurate or distorted
  • Persistent schemas shape personality consistency

Self-Schema (Self-Concept)

Definition: Beliefs about yourself; the most important schema

Components of Self-Schema

How Self-Schema Shapes Behavior

  1. Consistency: Act in ways that match self-image
  2. Selective attention: Notice information confirming self-view
  3. Interpretation: Interpret ambiguous events as supporting self-schema
  4. Memory: Remember things consistent with self-schema
  5. Goal selection: Choose activities matching self-concept

Self-Fulfilling Prophecy

Cognitive Processes in Personality

Attributions

  • Internal: "I failed because I'm stupid" (personality-based)
  • External: "I failed because the test was hard" (situation-based)
  • Determines: Self-esteem and future motivation

Biases

  • Confirmation bias: Seek info confirming existing beliefs
  • Negativity bias: Weight negative info more
  • Projection: Assume others think like us

Stress & Appraisal

  • Same stressor, different appraisals = different personality responses
  • "Challenge" vs. "threat" appraisal changes behavior
  • Personality shows through how we think about situations

Changing Personality Through Cognition

Cognitive Restructuring

  • Identify unhelpful thoughts
  • Challenge their accuracy
  • Replace with more accurate thoughts
  • Result: Personality changes

Example: Anxiety-Prone Personality

Before:

  • Thought: "Everyone judges me"
  • Behavior: Withdrawn, anxious
  • Personality: Shy, anxious

Restructuring:

  • Challenge: "Evidence they judge me?"
  • New thought: "Some people are friendly"
  • New behavior: Social attempts
  • New personality: More outgoing

Strengths & Limitations

Strengths

  • ✓ Testable and researched
  • ✓ Explains personality change
  • ✓ Clear intervention path (change thoughts → change personality)
  • ✓ Applies to depression, anxiety, personality disorders

Limitations

  • ✗ May overestimate conscious thought influence
  • ✗ Doesn't account for temperament/biology
  • ✗ Assumes rationality (people not always rational)
  • ✗ Ignores emotional and behavioral factors
