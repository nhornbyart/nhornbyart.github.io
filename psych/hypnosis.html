Hypnosis

Hypnosis

Definition: Narrowed focus of attention in which people become highly suggestible to changes in behavior and thought

How Hypnosis Works

Stages of Hypnosis

  1. Induction: Guide into hypnotic state
  2. Deepening: Increase depth of trance
  3. Therapeutic: Deliver suggestions
  4. Emergence: Gradually return to normal awareness

Posthypnotic Suggestion

Definition: Suggestion made during hypnosis that influences behavior after the session ends

How It Works

  • Suggestion planted in hypnotic state
  • Person wakes from hypnosis
  • Behavior/memory affected later without realizing why

Clinical Applications

Application Use
Memory recall Recover lost memories (though reliability debated)
Memory suppression Temporarily suppress painful memories
Habit breaking Reduce cravings, smoking cessation
Behavioral change Change automatic responses

Important Note

  • Controversial: Recovered memories may be false
  • Therapist responsibility: Ethical guidelines required
  • Not mind control: Can't make someone do something against values

Hypnotic Analgesia

Definition: Reduction of pain through hypnosis

Clinical Uses

  • Chronic pain management
  • Surgical procedures (in some settings)
  • Dental procedures
  • Burn treatment
  • Cancer pain (palliative care)

How It Works

  1. Relaxation reduces muscle tension
  2. Anxiety reduction = less pain amplification
  3. Altered attention to pain signals
  4. Psychological framing changes pain interpretation

Limitations

  • Works better for some people than others
  • Doesn't eliminate pain completely
  • Shouldn't replace medical treatment
  • Effect varies with susceptibility and condition
