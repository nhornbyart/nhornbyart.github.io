Personality Assessment Methods

Projective Tests

Definition: Tests where individuals interpret ambiguous stimuli, with responses revealing their unconscious personality, conflicts, and thought processes

How They Work

Assumptions

  • What we project reveals our mind
  • Unconscious shows through interpretation
  • No "right" answer = genuine response
  • Unique interpretations reveal personality

Rorschach Inkblot Test

Stimuli: 10 symmetrical inkblots

Procedure

  1. Show ambiguous inkblot
  2. Ask: "What might this be?"
  3. Note: What they see, where they see it, what details they use
  4. Repeat with 10 inkblots
  5. Analyze responses

What's Being Measured

Aspect What It Shows
Content Thought patterns, interests
Location Detail focus vs. big picture
Movement Energy, imagination
Color Emotional responsiveness
Popular Conformity, reality contact

Interpretation

  • Seeing "normal" things = good reality contact
  • Seeing movement = imagination, creativity
  • Excessive detail = obsessive thinking
  • Ignoring color = emotional withdrawal

Controversy

  • Supporters: Reveals unconscious processes
  • Critics: Low reliability, subjective scoring, limited predictive validity
  • Research: Mixed results; not widely validated

TAT (Thematic Apperception Test)

Stimuli: 30 ambiguous pictures (usually showing 1-3 people in unclear situation)

Procedure

  1. Show ambiguous picture
  2. Ask: "Tell a story about this picture"
  3. Prompt for: What's happening? What led to it? What will happen?
  4. Record full story
  5. Analyze themes across stories

What's Being Measured

Example Analysis

  • Themes of failure in stories = achievement anxiety
  • Lonely characters = isolation concerns
  • Aggressive scenarios = anger issues

Validity

  • Better researched than Rorschach
  • More interpretable stories (clear content)
  • Still subjective in scoring
  • Used clinically but not always validated

Common Issues with Projective Tests

Strengths

  • ✓ Open-ended (can't fake easily)
  • ✓ Reveals unconscious material
  • ✓ Rich qualitative data
  • ✓ Good clinical interview starters

Limitations

  • ✗ Low test-retest reliability
  • ✗ High scorer subjectivity
  • ✗ Difficult to quantify
  • ✗ Limited predictive validity
  • ✗ No strong empirical support for interpretations

Modern Use

  • Still used clinically
  • Often combined with other assessments
  • Better as exploratory than diagnostic tool
  • More common in therapeutic settings than research
