Sensory Thresholds

Absolute Threshold

Definition: The weakest amount of a stimulus required to produce a sensation

Key Fact

  • Detected 50% of the time at threshold
  • Below threshold: no sensation
  • Above threshold: sensation guaranteed

Real-World Examples

Sense Absolute Threshold
Vision Single candle flame from 30 km away
Hearing Ticking watch from 6 meters away
Taste 1 teaspoon of sugar in 7.5 liters of water
Smell 1 drop of perfume in a building
Touch Wing of a bee falling on cheek from 1 cm

Difference Threshold (Just Noticeable Difference - JND)

Definition: Minimum amount of difference a person can detect between two stimuli

Practical Application

  • Optometrist tests: Making you choose between slightly different lenses
  • Restaurant volume: Barely hear when someone turns up music slightly
  • Weight lifting: Can't tell difference between 100 lb and 101 lb, but can between 100 and 110 lb

Weber's Law

"The larger or stronger a stimulus, the greater the change needed to notice any difference"

Mathematical Relationship

K = ΔI / I

Where:

  • K = Constant (Weber's constant)
  • ΔI = Smallest detectable change
  • I = Original stimulus intensity

Implication

We perceive differences proportionally, not absolutely!
