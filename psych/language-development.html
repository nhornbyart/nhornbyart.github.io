Language Development

Timeline of Language Milestones

Learning Rate: Explosive Growth

Stages of Language Acquisition

Stage 1: Imitation

  • Baby hears words
  • Attempts to repeat sounds
  • Not yet understand meaning
  • Random trial and error

Stage 2: Overgeneralization

  • Child learns rule ("add -ed for past tense")
  • Over-applies rule: "goed", "eated"
  • Actually a sign of learning!
  • Example: All animals are "doggy"

Stage 3: Rule-Governed Language

  • Child learns exceptions
  • Uses grammar correctly
  • Flexible, creative use of language
  • Can understand novel sentences

Detailed Milestones

1 Year (12 months)

Behavior Example
Babbling baba, mama, dada
First word Mama, dog, ball
Understanding Knows own name
Gestures Points, waves

2 Years (24 months)

Behavior Example
Telegraphic speech "Mama up", "daddy go"
2-word phrases Function words missing
Questions Rising intonation: "Mommy go?"
Negation "No sleep", "No want"
Vocabulary ~50-300 words

3 Years (36 months)

Behavior Example
Sentences "I'm eating cookies"
Negation "I don't want that"
Grammar Subject-verb-object order
Accuracy ~80% grammatically correct
Vocabulary ~900-1000 words

4 Years (48 months)

Behavior Example
Grammar rules Past tense: "I played"
Future tense "I will go tomorrow"
Complex questions "Why is Tommy crying?"
Conversations Can hold dialogue
Vocabulary ~1500+ words

5 Years (60 months)

Behavior Example
Complex sentences "I see what you did"
Multiple clauses "Because it's raining, we can't play"
Storytelling Tells coherent stories
Conversations Mature dialogue
Vocabulary ~2000+ words

Key Debates

Nature vs. Nurture

Critical Period

Definition: Sensitive period early in life when language acquisition is easier

  • Peak: Birth to 3-5 years
  • Still possible: Beyond critical period, but harder
  • Bilingual advantage: Learning multiple languages easier during critical period
  • Later learning: Possible (like second language) but different process

Evidence:

  • Children with minimal language exposure struggle later
  • Second language accent harder to eliminate after 7-8 years
  • Early exposure helps native-like proficiency

What Supports Language Development

Parental Input

Strategy Effect
Talk to baby Provides input, models language
Read books Exposes to literacy, vocabulary
Respond to attempts Encourages more communication
Expand utterances Models correct grammar
Ask questions Stimulates thinking, language

Social Interaction

  • Conversation (not just TV) most effective
  • Back-and-forth interaction crucial
  • Emotional connection enhances learning

Bilingual Development

  • Learning two languages simultaneously possible
  • May have smaller vocabulary in each, but larger total
  • Bilingual advantages: Cognitive flexibility, metalinguistic awareness
