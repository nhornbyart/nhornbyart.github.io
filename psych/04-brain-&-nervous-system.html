04 Brain & Nervous System

Brain & Nervous System

The Nervous System

Function: Controls the body

Structure

Nervous System
├── Central Nervous System (CNS)
│   ├── Brain
│   └── Brain Stem
└── Peripheral Nervous System (PNS)
    └── Branches of nerves off to the sides

How Information Travels

  • Electrical signals: Throughout the neuron
  • Chemical signals: Neurotransmitters across the synapse

Neuron Components

Component Function
Nucleus Contains DNA
Cell Body Creates energy
Myelin Sheath Speeds up electrical impulses; protects the axon

Neurotransmitters

Definition: Chemicals produced in the cell

Factors Affecting Neurotransmitter Levels

  • Stress
  • Diet
  • Toxins
  • Genetics
  • Drugs
  • Alcohol
  • Caffeine

Oversupply and Undersupply Diseases

Oversupply

  • Acetylcholine → Alzheimer's disease
  • Dopamine → Schizophrenia

Undersupply

  • Dopamine → Parkinson's disease
  • Serotonin → Depression

Functions Controlled

  • Heartbeat
  • Digestion
  • Mood
  • Sleep
  • Concentration
  • Weight

Neurotransmitter Effects

Neurotransmitter Primary Effects
Serotonin Mood, sleep, pain, digestion
Dopamine Focus, motivation
Acetylcholine Movement, memory
Norepinephrine Anxiety, energy, sleep, focus
Epinephrine Heart rate, blood pressure

Synaptic Transmission

After release, neurotransmitters have two options:

  • Transmission: Information is passed to new cell
  • Inhibition: Signal stops

The Cerebral Cortex

Definition: The outer layer of the brain made of grey matter
Structure: Divided into four lobes

Temporal Lobe

Functions

  • Hearing
  • Some speech
  • Memory

Wernicke's Area

  • Function: Language comprehension
  • Damage result: Wernicke's Aphasia
    • Can speak well
    • Cannot understand what is being said
    • Produces "word salad"

Parietal Lobe

Functions

  • Sensory information (touch, temperature, pressure, etc.)
  • Visuospatial processing (your position in space)

Somatosensory Cortex

  • The larger area of the brain a body part takes up, the more sensitive the area is
  • Creates a distorted "sensory map"

Frontal Lobe

Functions

  • Planning
  • Reasoning
  • Movement (motor ability)
  • Personality
  • Working memory
  • Creative thinking and problem-solving

Motor Cortex

  • Key principle: The larger area of the brain a body part takes up, the more complex the movements are
  • Creates a distorted motor "map" with disproportionate space for hands, lips, tongue

Broca's Area

  • Function: Language production
  • Damage result: Can understand speech but cannot form words

Occipital Lobe

Functions

  • Visual processing
  • Processes: color, form, motion, location

Case Study: Phineas Gage

Significance: First case showing different functions are located in different parts of the brain

Details: Metal tamping iron went through his frontal lobe; resulted in personality and behavioral changes
