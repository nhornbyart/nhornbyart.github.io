Sleep Disorders

Sleep Apnea

Definition: One or more pauses in breathing or shallow breaths while sleeping

Characteristics

  • Person stops breathing repeatedly during sleep
  • Breathing resumes (usually with a gasp or snort)
  • May wake up partially without realizing

Consequences

  • Poor sleep quality
  • Daytime fatigue
  • Increased risk of heart problems
  • Can lead to sudden death if untreated

Types

  • Obstructive: Airways collapse during sleep
  • Central: Brain fails to signal breathing muscles
  • Complex: Combination of both

Insomnia

Definition: Persistent problems falling asleep and/or staying asleep

Causes

  • Stress and anxiety
  • Poor sleep habits
  • Medications
  • Sleep environment (noise, light, temperature)
  • Mental health conditions (depression, PTSD)
  • Caffeine, alcohol, nicotine

Effects

  • Daytime fatigue and irritability
  • Difficulty concentrating
  • Weakened immune system
  • Increased accident risk
  • Long-term health problems

Narcolepsy

Definition: Chronic, overwhelming daytime drowsiness caused by brain's inability to regulate sleep-wake cycles normally

Key Characteristics

  • Uncontrollable sleep attacks during day
  • Person may fall asleep during normal activities
  • Not simply tired — overwhelming need to sleep
  • REM sleep enters earlier than normal
  • May experience cataplexy (sudden loss of muscle tone triggered by emotion)

Biological Basis

  • Brain lacks adequate hypocretin/orexin (neurotransmitter regulating wakefulness)
  • Often caused by autoimmune condition destroying hypocretin neurons
  • Genetic component also involved

Management

  • Stimulant medications (modafinil, amphetamines)
  • Scheduled naps
  • Lifestyle modifications
  • Sodium oxybate (for cataplexy)
