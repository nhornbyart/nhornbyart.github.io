Photography Level 1

Projects are organized in chronological order, from earliest to most recent.

Chronological Projects

Manual Mode Challenge

A set of manual mode photos focusing on composition and exposure times. Black and white portrait, a flash shot, light painting (which was not required, but was really cool), a depth scene, a close‑up study, a strong foreground image, and a rule‑breaking photo.

Leading Lines

These shots use strong lines; like roads or fences; to pull your eye into the picture. Lines help make simple scenes feel more powerful and focused.

Rule of Thirds

These photos put the subject off to the side instead of the center. Moving things off-center usually makes pictures look more balanced and interesting.

Frame within a Frame

These shots use windows or doorways to frame the subject. The frame helps the main part of the photo stand out.

Viewpoint & Fill the Frame

Photos from different angles to show stance and shape. Close-up shots focus on details so subjects fill the frame.

Depth - Metal City

A tiny "city" made from staples, nails, and sand to study depth, scale, and texture.

Balance & Contrast

These photos show how elements balance and how light vs dark changes the feel. Some pictures are calm, others use strong contrast to grab attention.

Lenticular

Three lenticular frames showing slight changes in expression and angle. They flip when you view them from different sides.

Nature

A fall shot of Marshall Road in Michigan, showing the curving road framed by autumn trees. I wanted to capture the quiet mood and the color shift of the season.

Silhouettes

A silhouette of my friend Jeff against the sky. I wanted the shape and mood to tell the story.

Double Exposure

A double exposure that layers two scenes into one image. It plays with texture and mood by combining different subjects.

Storytelling

A short sequence with my friend Brady: he wants a drink from a vending machine, the machine fails and keeps his money, he gets angry and shakes it, then collapses from a heart attack. The series focuses on pacing and expression to make the story clear.

Practice Portraits

A practice portrait of my friend Alex using split lighting. His hat adds character and makes the shot feel cooler.

Triptych Portrait

A three-panel portrait of my friend Ethan. This was my least favorite; arranging the panels felt awkward.

Environmental Portraits

This project was not completed because there was not enough time due to other classes.
